# Disruption Scenarios — Detailed Analysis

This document provides extended context on each pre-configured disruption scenario in the REROUTE platform.

## Scenario 1: Strait of Hormuz Mining Operation

### Context

Iran has repeatedly threatened to close the Strait in response to Western sanctions or military action against its nuclear program. During the 1980s Iran-Iraq War, both nations attacked over 546 vessels in what became known as the "Tanker War."

### Attack Methodology

**Naval mine deployment** is Iran's most cost-effective denial strategy:
- **Influence mines**: Detonated by magnetic signature or acoustic profile of passing vessels
- **Contact mines**: Traditional floating mines anchored to seabed
- **Rocket mines**: Rise from seabed when target detected, explode in water column

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy (IRGCN) operates:
- ~10 mine-laying ships (converted commercial vessels)
- Fast attack craft with mine rails
- Midget submarines capable of covert deployment

### Historical Precedent

**Operation Earnest Will (1987-1988)**:
- U.S. Navy escorted reflagged Kuwaiti tankers through Persian Gulf
- USS Samuel B. Roberts struck mine (April 1988), causing $96M damage
- Operation Praying Mantis followed: U.S. destroyed 2 Iranian oil platforms and sank/damaged 6 Iranian vessels
- Mine-clearing took 4+ months with international coalition (U.S., UK, France, Belgium, Italy, Netherlands)

### Supply Impact Details

**Choke Point Physics**:
- Strait is 33km wide at narrowest point
- Only 2 shipping lanes (each 3km wide)
- Buffer zone of 3km between lanes
- Iranian territorial waters overlap northern lane
- Omani waters overlap southern lane

**Daily Throughput** (pre-crisis):
- Crude oil: ~17M bpd
- LNG: ~90 million tonnes per annum (mtpa) equivalent to ~3.5M bpd oil
- Refined products: ~0.2M bpd

**Countries Affected**:
- Saudi Arabia: 78% of exports transit Hormuz (rest via pipelines)
- UAE: 65% of exports (ADCOP pipeline bypasses strait)
- Iraq: 82% of exports (small volumes via Turkey pipeline)
- Kuwait: 95% of exports (no bypass option)
- Qatar: 92% of LNG exports (world's largest LNG exporter)
- Iran: 100% of exports blocked (by definition)

### Economic Impact Cascade

**Phase 1: Immediate (Days 0-5)**
- Oil price spike: 15-25% within 24 hours
- Futures markets move into steep backwardation (spot > forward)
- War risk insurance premiums: 0.05% → 3-5% of hull value
- Some vessels become uninsurable (Lloyd's market capacity exhausted)
- Charter rates for available VLCCs increase 40-60%

**Phase 2: Short-term Adjustment (Days 5-30)**
- IEA/US coordinate Strategic Petroleum Reserve release (~30-40M bbls over 30 days)
- OPEC+ increases production (limited by spare capacity: ~2-3M bpd)
- Asian refiners switch crude slate (quality mismatch → lower throughput)
- Cape route becomes viable but tanker shortage develops
- Demand destruction begins in emerging markets (subsidies removed, rationing)

**Phase 3: Medium-term (Days 30-90)**
- Saudi Petroline pipeline runs at 100% capacity
- UAE ADCOP pipeline at capacity
- Navy coalition begins mine-clearing operations
- Price stabilizes but 30-50% above pre-crisis
- Some industrial users (petrochemicals, manufacturing) idle capacity

### Geopolitical Responses

**Likely**:
- U.S. 5th Fleet based in Bahrain activates
- Aircraft carrier strike group(s) deployed
- Mine countermeasure vessels (MCM) from US, UK, France arrive
- UN Security Council emergency session (likely vetoed by Russia/China)
- Temporary truce negotiations via Oman as intermediary

**Possible**:
- U.S./Israel airstrikes on Iranian naval bases (Bandar Abbas, Bushehr)
- Iran retaliates against Saudi/Emirati oil infrastructure
- Ballistic missile strikes on Abqaiq, Ras Tanura, etc.
- Regional war escalation (Hezbollah, Houthis activate)
- Suez Canal also threatened by Iranian proxies

**Unlikely but catastrophic**:
- Nuclear facilities targeted
- Major ground invasion
- Use of weapons of mass destruction
- Global recession triggered by energy shock

## Scenario 2: Repeat Abqaiq-Khurais Attack

### 2019 Attack Analysis

On **September 14, 2019**, a swarm of drones and cruise missiles struck Saudi Aramco facilities:

**Targets**:
- Abqaiq processing plant: world's largest oil stabilization facility (7M bpd capacity)
- Khurais oil field: 1.5M bpd production

**Weapons**:
- 18x drones (Qasef-2K, Iranian design)
- 7x cruise missiles (variant of Iranian Soumar/Quds-1)
- Launched from Iran or Iraq (disputed)
- Total flight distance: ~900km
- Attack time: ~0400 local (minimal staff on-site)

**Damage Assessment**:
- 17 critical processing units hit
- Precision strikes (GPS-guided)
- 5.7M bpd offline (52% of Saudi production)
- Limited casualties (target was infrastructure, not personnel)

**Saudi Defense Failure**:
- Patriot missile batteries oriented toward Yemen (south), not Iran (north)
- Drones flew at low altitude, evaded radar
- Gulf War-era air defenses inadequate vs. modern UAVs

### Recovery Timeline (2019)

- **Day 1-2**: Aramco deploys emergency teams; extent of damage assessed
- **Day 3-10**: Temporary repairs using cannibalized parts; 50% capacity restored by Sep 24
- **Day 10-21**: Full capacity restoration announced Oct 11 (but questions about actual vs. claimed)

**Key Factors Enabling Fast Recovery**:
1. Saudi Arabia had spare parts inventory for critical components
2. International technical teams (Schlumberger, Halliburton) airlifted
3. Strategic petroleum stocks used to honor export contracts
4. Aramco's $2T valuation was at stake (IPO planned for Dec 2019)

### Why a Repeat Attack is Plausible

**Iranian Capability Improvements**:
- Shahed-136 "kamikaze drones" (>1000 produced, used in Ukraine war)
- Improved GPS/INS guidance (claimed CEP <10m)
- More autonomous swarm tactics (AI coordination)

**Saudi Defense Improvements**:
- Purchased additional air defense systems (MIM-104 Patriot upgrades, THAAD)
- Integrated early warning radar network
- Hardened critical infrastructure (blast walls, redundancy)

**But vulnerabilities remain**:
- Aramco operates 280+ facilities across Kingdom
- Cannot defend all simultaneously
- Automation/SCADA systems vulnerable to cyber pre-attack (disable defenses)

### 2024-2026 Attack Scenario

Assumes:
- Israel strikes Iranian nuclear facilities
- Iran retaliates against "US allies in region"
- Aramco's Abqaiq and/or Ras Tanura targeted
- Mix of drones, cruise missiles, and cyber attack

**Estimated Impact**:
- 5-8M bpd offline
- Recovery time: 14-30 days (slower than 2019 due to more extensive damage)
- Price spike: $135-150/bbl Brent
- Insurance war risk premiums apply to all Saudi exports

### Cascade Effects

**Refinery Supply Shock**:
- Asian refiners (India, China, Japan, South Korea) heavily dependent on Saudi Arab Medium/Heavy crude
- Cannot easily substitute with West African light sweet or North Sea Brent
- Refinery margins go negative (crude cost > product value)
- Some refineries reduce run rates 10-20%

**Petrochemical Impact**:
- Abqaiq processes associated gas → ethane, propane feedstock
- Global ethylene/propylene markets disrupted
- Plastics, solvents, industrial chemicals affected
- Supply chains for automotive, packaging, construction impacted

**SPR Release Dynamics**:
- U.S. has released from SPR twice in recent years:
  - 2022: 180M bbls over 6 months (Russia-Ukraine war)
  - Reserves now ~370M bbls (vs. historical peak 727M bbls)
- Release capacity: ~4.4M bpd max (limited by pipeline infrastructure from salt caverns)
- Takes 13-15 days to reach market
- IEA coordination: members hold 1.2B bbls total

## Scenario 3: Iran-Israel Regional Conflict (Worst Case)

This represents the **"gray rhino"** — high impact, high probability, but ignored because it's uncomfortable to model.

### Escalation Pathway

**Trigger Event** (could be any of):
1. Israel strikes Iranian Natanz/Fordow nuclear facilities
2. Iran crosses weapons-grade enrichment threshold (90%+ U-235)
3. Assassination of senior Iranian nuclear scientist or IRGC commander
4. Iranian proxy attack kills significant Israeli/US personnel
5. Miscalculation during "shadow war" (ship attacks, cyber warfare, drone incidents)

**Days 0-3**:
- Massive ballistic missile exchange (Iran launches 200+ missiles at Israel; Iron Dome saturated)
- Israeli Air Force conducts strikes on Iranian missile launch sites, IRGC bases
- Hezbollah enters war from Lebanon (150k+ rockets; northern Israel evacuated)
- US bases in Iraq, Syria, Kuwait, Bahrain attacked by Iranian proxies
- IRGCN fast attack craft swarm commercial vessels in Hormuz
- Underwater mine deployment begins

**Days 3-7**:
- US declares Hormuz closed to commercial traffic pending mine clearance
- Iran declares "war zone" extending 200nm from coast
- Saudi/Emirati oil infrastructure struck (drones, cruise missiles, sabotage)
- Global oil market in panic; trading halts trigger on futures exchanges
- Emergency IEA meeting; 60M bbl SPR release authorized

**Days 7-30**:
- Ongoing air campaign against Iranian military targets
- Ground operations in southern Lebanon (Israeli incursion)
- Iraqi militias attack US troops; consider full US withdrawal
- Houthi attacks in Red Sea intensify (anti-ship ballistic missiles)
- Suez traffic down 70%+
- Global recession begins (energy shock + supply chain paralysis)

### Military-Technical Realities

**Iranian Anti-Access/Area Denial (A2/AD)**:
- 2,000+ ballistic missiles (Shahab-3, Sejil, Khorramshahr)
- 3,000+ cruise missiles and drones
- Anti-ship cruise missiles (Noor/C-802, Zafar, Ghader) with 200km range
- Anti-ship ballistic missiles (Hormuz-2) experimental but demonstrated
- Coastal defense cruise missile batteries cover entire Strait
- Mini-submarines, fast attack craft, mines

**US 5th Fleet Capabilities**:
- 1-2 carrier strike groups (90 aircraft, Aegis cruisers/destroyers)
- B-52/B-1B strategic bombers from Diego Garcia or CONUS
- F-22/F-35 stealth fighters from Qatar, UAE
- Mine countermeasure ships (limited number, slow operation)
- TLAM (Tomahawk Land Attack Missiles) strikes on Iranian coastal defenses

**Operational Reality**:
- US can establish air superiority but not invulnerable
- Iran can impose unacceptable costs (not "win" but deny access)
- Mine clearance: 4-8 weeks minimum with full international coalition
- Some tankers will be hit (insurance becomes impossible)

### Supply Disruption Modeling

**Assume**:
- Hormuz: 95% closure (19.8M bpd disrupted)
- Bab el-Mandeb: 70% closure (Houthis active; 4.3M bpd disrupted)
- Saudi pipelines: operating but vulnerable (7M bpd bypass capacity partially utilized)
- Iraq-Turkey pipeline: offline (Kurdish politics + Iran pressure on Baghdad)

**Net Global Supply Loss**: ~18-22M bpd (depending on pipeline utilization, sabotage success)

**Demand Side**:
- Price spike → demand destruction of ~2-3M bpd (price elasticity -0.15)
- Recession-induced demand loss: ~1-2M bpd additional (6-12 month lag)

**Supply Response**:
- OPEC+ spare capacity: ~2.5M bpd (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Iraq, Russia)
- US shale response: slow (takes 6-9 months to drill new wells)
- SPR releases: 4M bpd for ~30 days (then exhausted)

**Arithmetic**:
- Loss: 20M bpd
- Offset: 2.5 (OPEC spare) + 4 (SPR) + 2.5 (demand destruction) = 9M bpd
- **Gap: 11M bpd shortfall**

**Price Implications**:
- Simple supply elasticity suggests $180-220/bbl
- Economic contraction would eventually reduce demand further
- But: **liquidity crisis** as energy-importing nations face forex shortages
- Rationing, price controls, black markets emerge

## Scenario 4: Red Sea Extended Closure

### Background: 2024-2025 Houthi Campaign

Following October 2023 Israel-Gaza war, Yemen's Houthi movement (backed by Iran) launched attacks on commercial shipping:

**Pattern**:
- Anti-ship ballistic missiles (ASBMs) fired at transiting vessels
- Drone boats (USVs) packed with explosives
- Helicopter-deployed boarding teams attempt hijackings
- ~60+ attacks by mid-2025

**Response**:
- US Operation Prosperity Guardian (naval coalition)
- Airstrikes on Houthi launch sites in Yemen
- Commercial vessels reroute via Cape of Good Hope
- Suez Canal revenue down 60%+
- Marine insurance war risk premiums apply to Red Sea transit

### Extended Scenario (2026-2027)

**Assumptions**:
- Houthi capabilities continue to improve (Iranian supply lines)
- Western fatigue with Middle East conflicts → reduced naval presence
- One or more high-profile sinkings or mass-casualty events
- Insurers withdraw coverage entirely for Red Sea

**Result**:
- Suez Canal utilization: <35% of normal (only vessels willing to accept risk)
- Average 4.2M bpd oil + 35 mtpa LNG rerouted via Cape
- Egypt loses $8-10B annually in canal revenue (major economic crisis)

### Route Economics

**Suez Route** (Middle East → Europe):
- Distance: ~6,500 nautical miles
- Time: 12-15 days
- Suez toll: $450k (VLCC northbound)
- Total voyage cost: ~$900k-$1.1M

**Cape Route**:
- Distance: ~13,000 nautical miles
- Time: 28-30 days
- No canal toll
- Total voyage cost: ~$1.5M-$2.1M (15 extra days × $42k/day charter + fuel)

**Cost Per Barrel**:
- Suez: $0.45-0.55/bbl
- Cape: $0.75-1.05/bbl
- **Premium: $0.30-0.50/bbl sustained**

### Second-Order Effects

**Tanker Market Tightness**:
- Global VLCC fleet: ~800 vessels
- Average utilization: 85%
- Each Cape rerouting ties up vessel for +15 days
- Effective capacity reduction: ~12-15%
- Charter rates spike 40-60%
- Scrapping of older vessels delayed (high demand)

**Refinery Crude Slate Issues**:
- European refineries optimized for Urals (Russian crude, now sanctioned) + Arab Medium
- West African alternatives (light sweet) require different processing
- Refinery margins compressed
- Some refineries unprofitable, reduce run rates

**LNG Market Fragmentation**:
- European LNG imports (replacing Russian pipeline gas) face delays
- Qatari LNG to Europe +15 days = +$1-2/MMBtu freight cost
- US Gulf Coast LNG exports become more competitive (Atlantic crossing avoids Red Sea)

**Geopolitical**:
- Egypt's economy collapses without Suez revenue
- IMF bailout required (political instability)
- China leverages Belt & Road to build alternative logistics (land routes through Central Asia)

## Scenario 5: Baseline (No Major Disruption)

### "Normal" Isn't Risk-Free

Even in peacetime, the global oil market faces:

**Chronic Issues**:
- OPEC+ production management (voluntary cuts to support prices)
- US-Iran sanctions (1M bpd Iranian exports offline)
- Venezuelan collapse (3M bpd lost since 2015)
- Libya civil war (intermittent disruptions, 0.5-1.2M bpd)
- Nigerian pipeline sabotage (100-300k bpd losses)
- Russian export shifts due to sanctions (rerouting from Europe to Asia)

**Price Range**:
- Brent: $75-95/bbl typical (2023-2026 average)
- Volatility driven by:
  - US EIA inventory reports (weekly)
  - OPEC+ meeting outcomes (monthly-ish)
  - Geopolitical headlines (daily)
  - Speculative positioning in futures markets

**Embedded Risk Premium**:
- Baseline price includes ~$5-10/bbl geopolitical risk premium
- Reflects probability-weighted expected value of disruptions
- "Iran risk premium" specifically ~$3-5/bbl

### Business-as-Usual Challenges

**For Importers (refiners, traders)**:
- Crude slate optimization (blend different grades to match refinery configuration)
- Freight rate volatility (charter markets fluctuate 30-50% month-to-month)
- Contract negotiations (annual supply agreements with FOB/CIF terms)
- Inventory management (carry costs vs. shortage risk)

**For Exporters (NOCs)**:
- Production quotas (OPEC+ compliance vs. revenue needs)
- Infrastructure maintenance (turnarounds, brownfield investment)
- Market access (securing long-term offtake agreements)
- Price hedging (futures, swaps, options to lock in revenue)

## Cross-Scenario Insights

### Common Patterns

1. **Price spikes are short-lived but severe**: Historical disruptions show 15-35% immediate spike, normalization over 4-12 weeks
2. **Spare capacity is key**: OPEC+ buffer determines how much supply can be replaced
3. **SPR releases lag**: 13-15 days from announcement to market; political process adds delay
4. **Freight is underestimated**: Voyage costs can exceed crude price changes (especially for LNG)
5. **Demand destruction works**: High prices reduce consumption; elasticity kicks in after 2-4 weeks

### Risk Multipliers

Disruptions are worse when:
- **Multiple chokepoints affected** (Hormuz + Red Sea = global gridlock)
- **Low inventory levels** (OECD commercial stocks <60 days cover = vulnerable)
- **Tight spare capacity** (<2M bpd OPEC+ buffer = no relief valve)
- **Winter demand peak** (heating oil, LNG demand in Northern Hemisphere)
- **Geopolitical alignment** (Russia-Iran-China cooperation amplifies Western vulnerability)
